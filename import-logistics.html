The Challenge

Why Importing Into South Africa Is More Complex Than It Should Be

International freight and South African customs clearance involve multiple parties, documentation requirements, and regulatory compliance that most businesses are not equipped to manage alone.

Complex Customs Documentation
South African customs requires precise documentation — commercial invoices, packing lists, bills of lading, permits, and tariff codes — with errors causing holds and costly delays at port.
Unexpected Delays at Port
Goods held at Durban or Cape Town port for incorrect or incomplete documentation result in demurrage charges and supply chain disruption.
Incorrect Duty and Tax Classification
Incorrect tariff code classification leads to overpaying duties or triggering SARS customs investigations — both of which are costly and time-consuming to resolve.
No Visibility Over Shipment Status
Without a dedicated logistics manager, importers often have no real-time visibility over their shipment — not knowing where goods are or when they will arrive.
Regulatory and Permit Requirements
Certain product categories require ITAC permits, SABS/NRCS approvals, or other regulatory clearances before goods can be released — requirements many importers are caught off guard by.
Fragmented Freight Relationships
Managing separate relationships with freight forwarders, clearing agents, and local carriers creates accountability gaps and makes it difficult to resolve issues when things go wrong.
